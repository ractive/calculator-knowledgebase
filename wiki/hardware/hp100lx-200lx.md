# HP 100LX / HP 200LX

HP's MS-DOS palmtops, successors of the 95LX. This wiki covers them only as
far as their cable and IR links go; they have no Saturn chip. Their
built-in Filer speaks the [[protocols/filer-hplx]] protocol, and DataComm
offers Kermit and XModem.

## Summary

| Item | 100LX / 200LX |
| --- | --- |
| Dates | 100LX ROM 1.01a in units with serial prefix SG3xx (1993); 200LX released "around the first of 1995" (src: [[sources/palmtops-faq]] ROM versions, 200LX answer) |
| Differences | hardware "virtually identical" (serial port, PCMCIA, display, CPU, RAM); the 200LX changes software (src: [[sources/palmtops-faq]]) |
| System | 80186-class CPU, MS-DOS 5.00 in ROM, CGA, 640 KB system RAM; 1 MB and 2 MB models, the extra MB going to the C: disk (src: [[sources/palmtops-faq]] MSD listing of a 1 MB 100LX; 1MB vs 2MB) |
| Drives | C: internal RAM disk, D: ROM (holds `D:\BIN`, `D:\DOS\COMMAND.COM`), A: the PCMCIA card (src: [[sources/palmtops-faq]]; [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P5) |
| Serial port | one COM1 at I/O 0x3F8 with an 8250 UART (src: [[sources/palmtops-faq]] MSD listing); a standard port with hardware handshaking, unlike the 95LX's (src: [[sources/palmtops-faq]] 95LX comparison) |

| ROM versions | 100LX 1.01a to 1.06a; 200LX 1.00a to 1.02a (as of 1996) (src: [[sources/palmtops-faq]]) |

## Connector and HP's cable

- The 100LX introduced a small 10-pin serial connector with 2 mm pin
  pitch (IDE-drive spacing, not 0.1 inch); a connector of the wrong pitch
  can break a pin. Ten pins because the palmtop side has two ground pins
  (src: [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P1).
- HP's PC cable is the F1015A (cable part F1015-80002), about a metre,
  with a ferrite core near the palmtop end that is only slipped over the
  insulation. The PC end is a female DE9 (DB9) with the null modem built
  in, made to plug straight into a PC's male DE9 port (src:
  [[sources/palmtops-faq]] section 12; [[sources/hp200lx-faq]];
  [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P1; [[sources/holyjoe-cable-table]]). The Mac
  version is the F1016A (F1016-80002) (src: [[sources/holyjoe-cable-table]]).
- The cable comes with an adaptor (5181-6643) from its 10-pin end to the
  4-pin plug of the 95LX and the HP 48. The original 4-pin 95LX/48 cables
  cannot be converted for the 100LX (src: [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P1;
  [[sources/holyjoe-cable-table]]; [[sources/palmtops-faq]]).
- The palmtop plug has "WIDE" keying, a bump meant for the palmtops only
  (src: [[sources/holyjoe-cable-table]] keying note). Insert it with the
  bump and the HP logo facing up; it goes in upside down too, and then
  nothing connects (src: [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P6).
- Connectivity Pack: F1021A for the 100LX, F1021B/F1021C for the 200LX,
  with the cable, the adapter kit F1023A and PC software (src:
  [[sources/palmtops-faq]] section 12). The kit's four adaptors turn the
  cable into a male DB25 or female DB25 with null modem, or a male DB25 or
  male DE9 without (src: [[sources/palmtops-faq]]). For a PC connection an
  adaptor must keep pins 2 and 3 crossed: 5181-6641 (computer icon) and
  5181-6640 (printer icon) do (src: [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P6).
- **USB serial adapter.** An ordinary USB serial adapter has a male DB9 in
  the role of a PC port, so the stock cable plugs straight into it: no
  extra null modem, no gender changer (inference from the cable being made
  for a PC's male DE9). The Filer link uses no handshake lines
  ([[protocols/filer-hplx]] "Physical layer"); an adapter that holds DTR
  and RTS high does no harm, since HP's own PC driver keeps both on (src:
  [[sources/hplx-connectivity-pack]] C2).

### Cable warnings

- **The palmtop cable on a 49G destroys the 49G.** The F1015-80002 and
  F1016-80002 plugs fit the 49G's 10-pin port, and the cable "fries it"
  (src: [[sources/holyjoe-cable-table]]). See [[hardware/hp49g]].
- **No 38G/49G cable or adaptor on the palmtop.** The 38G and 49G cables
  and adaptors (F1897-66000, F1906-66000, 1252-6635 / CONNE-80003) are
  listed for those calculators, with "small" or no keying (src:
  [[sources/holyjoe-cable-table]]). Since the palmtop cable destroys a
  49G, the two 10-pin pinouts must differ, so a calculator cable or
  adaptor should not be tried on the palmtop either (inference; no source
  states a damage case in this direction).

## Speeds

- Filer's Remote Settings go up to 115,200 baud for a link to another
  100/200LX (src: [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P2, P3); the Connectivity
  Pack default is 9600 (src: [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P4).
- Scardina: 300 bps to 115 kbps between palmtop and PC, file-transfer
  programs reach the top rates; an efficient DOS program with the System
  Manager not loaded "should be able to easily communicate at 38.4K bps",
  with it loaded half that, improved in 100LX ROM 1.05A (src:
  [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P1).
- The FAQ: the stock internal 8250 is good for 19200; up to 115200 works
  only for receiving, through a buffered UART (16550) on a PCMCIA card;
  38400 with a double-speed crystal (src: [[sources/palmtops-faq]] "How
  fast is the highest speed for serial port?").
- Reports with the Filer protocol: lxbackup defaults to 38400 and advises
  19200 when transfers fail (src: [[sources/lxtools]] L2); LX Tools 1.1b
  added 57600 and 115200 and retries for errors seen at high speed (src:
  [[sources/lxtools]] L1); BSD Filer ran with a 200LX at 38400 by IR and
  9600 by cable, LXFS4D at 57600 by IR and by cable (src:
  [[sources/bsd-filer]] B1, B3).

## Serial set-up for Filer

How HP's PC programs for the 100LX set up the serial port for a Filer
link, read in their code (src: [[sources/hplx-connectivity-pack]] C1,
C2). The palmtop's Filer is the same code built for the palmtop, so the
same settings are inferred for its side.

- Speeds offered: 1200, 2400, 4800, 9600, 19200, 38400, 57600, 115200,
  as divisors 96, 48, 24, 12, 6, 3, 2 and 1 of a 1.8432 MHz UART clock.
  300 baud (divisor 384) is known to the timing table but not offered.
- 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit; no flow control (XON/XOFF and
  RTS/CTS both off, though the driver supports them for other uses).
- DTR and RTS on for the whole link (modem control 0x0F: DTR, RTS, OUT1,
  OUT2); CTS, DSR and DCD ignored, so three wires suffice. The UART's
  previous settings are restored when the port closes.
- The FIFO is never switched on: the UART runs as a plain 8250. At 19200
  and above the PC driver keeps polling the UART for a while after each
  interrupt so that bytes arriving back to back are not lost.
- COM1 at 0x3F8 on IRQ4, COM2 at 0x2F8 on IRQ3 (the PC's ports).
- For the infrared interface (inferred) the driver has a half-duplex
  mode: after sending it waits until the last bit is out, throws away the
  echo it receives (about 50 bytes) and then listens again.

## Filer server mode

- In Filer: MENU, Communications, Remote Settings. The dialog holds Baud,
  Interface (Com1 for the cable, Infrared for IR), Phone and Server Mode;
  set Server Mode to enabled and confirm with OK (F10). The Connectivity
  Pack defaults are 9600, Com1, no phone number, server mode enabled (src:
  [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P4; [[sources/lxtools]] L2; [[sources/bsd-filer]]
  B1). COM2 and "Alternate" do not work with the Connectivity Pack (src:
  [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P6).
- With server mode enabled and Filer open, a PC client needs nothing else
  on the palmtop (src: [[sources/lxtools]] L1, L2). While serving, the
  palmtop can do nothing but end server mode (src: [[sources/bsd-filer]]
  B1).
- The palmtop as client: in Filer, F7 splits the screen, F6 (Remote)
  connects, F10 disconnects; the remote side can be another palmtop, HP's
  desktop Filer from the Connectivity Pack, or a server emulator (src:
  [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P2, P4; [[sources/bsd-filer]] B1).
- Switching to another application during a link and coming back can end
  in "Remote Connection Broken"; this happens with the Connectivity Pack
  too (src: [[sources/bsd-filer]] B1). HP's client shows that text for
  every link failure, including no answer to a request after three tries
  (src: [[sources/hplx-connectivity-pack]] C1).
- With server mode off, Filer ignores a connect; HP's PC client gives up
  after about 12 s with "Cannot establish a remote connection" (src:
  [[sources/hplx-connectivity-pack]] C1; inferred for the palmtop).
- Auto-off: HP's client sends nothing while idle, so it does nothing to
  keep the palmtop awake. The Filer code holds a call to the palmtop
  BIOS's auto-off timer and a routine that powers the serial port, both
  unused or empty in the PC build; that the palmtop's own build uses them
  to stay on while serving is inferred, not confirmed (src:
  [[sources/hplx-connectivity-pack]] C1).
- The 95LX's Filer links to a 100LX over IR at 2400 baud (src:
  [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P2, P3). Whether its command set equals the
  100LX's is not documented.

## DataComm: Kermit and XModem

- The 200LX's DataComm supports XModem, YModem, ZModem and Kermit (src:
  [[sources/hp200lx-faq]]). For the 100LX only Kermit is documented in the
  sources read (src: [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P5).
- DataComm has a Kermit server: MENU, Connect, Kermit Commands, then the
  option that puts the palmtop into server mode. The other side then
  gets files by full DOS path (`C:\_DAT\MYPHONE.PDB`) or with a wildcard
  (`C:\*.*`); subdirectories are not included and must be fetched one by
  one. The example uses 19200 baud, Com1, no parity, 8 data bits, 1 stop
  bit, set under MENU, Connect, Settings (src: [[sources/palmtop-paper]]
  P5).
- On the 100LX, DataComm is very slow as a terminal but reasonably fast for
  file transfers; the 200LX lacks the slow screen update (src:
  [[sources/palmtops-faq]]). MS-Kermit also runs on the palmtops (src:
  [[sources/palmtops-faq]]; [[sources/hp200lx-faq]]).

## On a real 100LX (2026-11-09)

The owner's 100LX with a German ROM (observations on real hardware by
the owner and Claude, 2026-11-09):

- A client following [[protocols/filer-hplx]] connected to Filer in
  server mode over HP's cable and a USB serial adapter, listed
  directories and copied files.
- With 1-2-3 open and a spreadsheet loaded, DataComm and "Prog manager"
  refused to start with "Zuwenig Speicherplatz" (not enough memory), and
  the &... key only beeped. After 1-2-3 was closed, DataComm started.
  The files on C: had nothing to do with it.

## Self-test of the serial port

TEST mode: with the palmtop off, hold ESC and press ON. "Wire serial port"
shows "no loop" after about two seconds when the port is healthy and no
loopback plug is fitted; HP's support used this to free a locked-up port,
which a reboot does not (src: [[sources/palmtop-paper]] P6).

## Contradictions

- The highest safe speed: 19200 for the stock UART (FAQ) against Filer
  settings up to 115,200 and reported Filer links at 38400 and 57600. See
  [[questions/hplx-serial-speed]].
- The FAQ calls the 100/200LX port a standard port with hardware
  handshaking, while the Filer protocol runs without any flow control
  ([[protocols/filer-hplx]]); not a contradiction about the hardware:
  HP's own driver supports RTS/CTS but switches it off for Filer (src:
  [[sources/hplx-connectivity-pack]] C2).
