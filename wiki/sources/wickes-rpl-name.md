# Wickes: what RPL stands for (1991)

A short note by Bill Wickes, HP Corvallis, dated 11 March 1991, on
hpcalc.org as `rpl.txt` (details page <https://www.hpcalc.org/details/6852>;
file <https://www.hpcalc.org/hp48/docs/programming/rpl.txt>). Not in
`raw/`; under 1 KB, read on 2026-10-12. Wickes led the HP-28C project, so
this is a primary source on the name.

## What it says

- RPL stands for **Reverse Polish Lisp**. The development team coined it
  for the then unnamed system, as a play on RPN and as a pointer to the
  language's descent from Forth and Lisp.
- **"ROM-based procedural language"** was a later, more formal reading of
  the same initials, made up around the HP Journal article on the HP-28C
  (August 1987), because RPL had never been meant as a public name.
- The team itself only ever said "RPL"; Wickes leaves the reader to pick
  either expansion.

A longer statement of the same history, from Wickes' "RPL: A Mathematical
Control Language" (Programming Environments, Institute for Applied Forth
Research, 1988), as quoted by Joseph Horn on comp.sys.hp48: the new
system merges Forth's threaded interpretation with Lisp's functional
approach, is known unofficially as RPL for Reverse-Polish Lisp, and first
shipped in the HP-18C in June 1986 (src: [[sources/comp-sys-hp48-archive]]
C38).
